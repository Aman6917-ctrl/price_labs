# Pricing Best Practices

Guidance support engineers can share with hosts during onboarding and optimization reviews.

## Start with clean inputs

1. Correct bedroom/bathroom counts and location pin.
2. Realistic base price from last 90 days of booked ADR (similar LOS).
3. Sensible min/max (min often 60–75% of base; max 150–250% depending on market).
4. Connect the primary booking channel before enabling auto-push.

## Roll out auto-push carefully

- Week 1: recommendations only — host reviews daily.
- Week 2: auto-push for far-out dates (30+ days) only.
- Week 3: enable full auto-push if variance looks healthy.

## Use seasons intentionally

- Peak seasons: raise mins, keep dynamic within bounds.
- Off-season: lower mins slightly; let last-minute factor work.
- Avoid dozens of micro-seasons — they confuse hosts and support.

## Comp sets

Comps are context, not a mandate. Prefer comps with similar:
- Capacity and bedroom count
- Amenity quality
- Distance / neighborhood

If all comps are luxury villas and the listing is a studio, market signals will mislead.

## Review cadence

| Cadence | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Weekly | Scan next 30 days for odd cliffs or stuck mins |
| Monthly | Revisit base price vs. booked ADR |
| Quarterly | Reassess seasons and LOS ladder |

## What not to do

- Do not set min = max (disables dynamic pricing).
- Do not run two dynamic pricing tools on the same channel listing.
- Do not lock the entire calendar "just in case" — it blocks the product value.

## Measuring success

Track booking pace, occupancy, and ADR vs. market — not only whether prices "look high." Support should steer conversations toward outcomes, not single-night sticker shock.
